01 / THE BEGINNINGS1950s–1960s

THE FIRST
DIGITAL GAMES.

Before home consoles, researchers experimented with interactive electronics. Tennis for Two turned an oscilloscope into a simple tennis game, and Spacewar! brought a space battle to a university computer.

Tennis for Two · 1958Spacewar! · 1962

THE SHIFT / Computers became places people could play, not only machines for calculations.

02 / THE ARCADE ERA1972–late 1970s

ARCADES
LIGHT UP.

Pong helped make video games a public attraction. Soon, arcade cabinets brought games into cafés, shops and dedicated game rooms, while Space Invaders turned high scores into a shared obsession.

Magnavox Odyssey · 1972Pong · 1972Space Invaders · 1978

THE SHIFT / Gaming moved from research labs into public entertainment.

03 / HOME CONSOLESLate 1970s–early 1980s

THE GAME
COMES HOME.

Cartridge-based systems made it possible to play different games on one console. The Atari 2600 helped turn the television into a home gaming screen, while Intellivision brought another major system into the living room.

Atari 2600 · 1977Intellivision · 1979Interchangeable cartridges

THE SHIFT / Players could build a collection and choose what to play at home.

04 / 8-BIT & 16-BIT1983–late 1980s

CHARACTERS
COME ALIVE.

Nintendo's Family Computer launched in Japan in 1983 and reached North America as the NES in 1985. Sega's Mega Drive arrived in Japan in 1988. More expressive graphics helped games build recognizable worlds and characters.

Famicom · Japan, 1983NES · North America, 1985Mega Drive · Japan, 1988Super Mario Bros. · 1985

THE SHIFT / Franchises, memorable characters and console rivalries became central to gaming.

05 / THE 3D REVOLUTION1990–2000

A WHOLE
NEW DIMENSION.

The Super Nintendo era refined 2D worlds, while the PlayStation and Nintendo 64 helped bring polygonal 3D environments into the mainstream. Players could explore spaces that felt larger and more spatial.

Super Famicom · Japan, 1990PlayStation · Japan, 1994Nintendo 64 · 1996

THE SHIFT / Disc storage, 3D graphics and new camera perspectives changed game design.

06 / ONLINE GAMING2001–2005

WORLDS
CONNECT.

PlayStation 2 and Xbox pushed console hardware forward. Online services and broadband connections made it easier for players to compete and cooperate across distances.

PlayStation 2 · 2000Xbox · 2001Online multiplayer expands

THE SHIFT / Multiplayer could reach beyond the same room and the same screen.

07 / HIGH DEFINITION2005–2010

DETAILS
GET SHARPER.

Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 brought high-definition graphics to living-room consoles, while PC gaming continued pushing visual detail and performance. Wii added motion controls and online stores widened how players connected.

Xbox 360 · 2005PlayStation 3 · 2006PC gaming · HD graphics

THE SHIFT / HD visuals grew alongside motion controls and connected communities.

08 / NEW GENERATIONS2011–2015

A MORE
CONNECTED ERA.

PlayStation 4 and Xbox One arrived in 2013. Digital libraries, downloadable games, streaming features and large online communities became a normal part of console ecosystems.

PlayStation 4 · 2013Xbox One · 2013Digital libraries grow

THE SHIFT / Discovering, sharing and updating games increasingly happened online.

09 / REALISM & ESPORTS2016–2020

NEW WAYS
TO PLAY.

PlayStation VR brought console-focused virtual reality to more players, while Nintendo Switch combined handheld and TV play. Competitive gaming and esports also reached wider audiences.

PlayStation VR · 2016Nintendo Switch · 2017VR & esports grow

THE SHIFT / Gaming expanded beyond the traditional screen and living-room setup.

10 / NEW-GENERATION CONSOLES2020–2023

SPEED MEETS
DETAIL.

PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S launched in 2020. Solid-state storage, advanced lighting and stronger hardware helped developers create detailed worlds with shorter loading times.

PlayStation 5 · 2020Xbox Series X|S · 2020SSD & ray tracing

THE SHIFT / Faster storage and richer rendering became defining parts of the generation.

11 / THE FUTURE OF GAMING2024 onward

THE NEXT
FRONTIER.

Cloud gaming, AI-assisted development, mixed reality and new graphics techniques are active areas of experimentation. Their long-term impact is still unfolding, so the next chapter remains an open question.

Cloud gamingAI-assisted toolsImmersive interfaces

THE SHIFT / These are developing technologies and trends, not guaranteed predictions.

05 / A SURPRISING SONIC STORY

DID YOU KNOW?

A pop-music legend, Sega’s blue hedgehog and one of the most talked-about stories behind a 16-bit soundtrack.

MICHAEL JACKSON × SONIC

MICHAEL JACKSON WAS A HUGE SONIC FAN.

Former Sega Technical Institute director Roger Hector described Michael Jackson as a “very big fan of Sonic” and said he visited the team while Sonic the Hedgehog 3 was being developed.

Jackson’s longtime musical collaborator Brad Buxer later said that he and Jackson worked on music for Sonic the Hedgehog 3, released for the Sega Genesis / Mega Drive in 1994.

The story is still debated: Sega never officially confirmed Jackson’s contribution, and later accounts differed. That uncertainty is part of what makes this such a fascinating piece of gaming history.
